Overview

Robots frequently encounter furniture, carts, boxes, and other large objects that are difficult or impractical to grasp. Non-prehensile manipulation offers a practical alternative: the robot can use its body, legs, and arm contacts to push, slide, and reposition objects toward a goal.

The preliminary results show that a hierarchical controller can coordinate a legged robot and arm to push diverse objects in simulation and in initial real-world trials.

System Overview

The high-level policy selects leg and arm commands for pushing. A pre-trained locomotion controller tracks the leg command and maintains stable motion, while the arm command creates useful object contact. The belief module summarizes recent interaction so the policy can respond to object motion, contact quality, friction, mass, and slip without relying on privileged state at deployment.
